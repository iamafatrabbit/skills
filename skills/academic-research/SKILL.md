# Academic Research

## Purpose
Help turn a research idea into a transparent, evidence-based academic workflow. Prioritize reliable sources, clear distinctions between evidence and interpretation, and reproducible notes.

## Workflow

1. Clarify the question
   - Restate the research question, scope, population, time period, and intended output.
   - Identify key concepts and useful search terms in English and Chinese when relevant.

2. Plan the evidence search
   - Prefer primary studies, official statistics, academic journals, and systematic reviews.
   - Record databases searched, search terms, date range, and inclusion/exclusion rules.

3. Evaluate sources
   - Note author, publication date, venue, method, sample/data, key finding, and limitations.
   - Do not treat abstracts, social posts, or unsourced claims as conclusive evidence.

4. Synthesize rather than list
   - Group findings by theme, method, or result.
   - Clearly label consensus, disagreement, uncertainty, and gaps in the literature.

5. Produce the deliverable
   - Use a logical structure: question → method → evidence → interpretation → limitations → next steps.
   - Include traceable citations or links for factual claims.

## Quality Checks

- Separate direct evidence from inference.
- State assumptions and limitations plainly.
- Never invent citations, quotations, results, or page numbers.
- Flag when information may be outdated or insufficient.
- For quantitative claims, verify units, dates, definitions, and comparability.

## Output Templates

### Literature-review table
| Source | Question / method | Key finding | Limitation | Relevance |
|---|---|---|---|---|

### Research brief
1. Research question
2. What the evidence says
3. Conflicting evidence and limitations
4. Implications
5. Recommended next research step

- Summarize the literature on AI and labor-market polarization since 2020.
- Build a reproducible search plan for the effect of minimum wages on employment.
- Turn these papers into a structured literature-review table.
